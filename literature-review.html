Chapter 4

Literature Review

Organized by theme rather than source-by-source.

1

Romantic Relationships During Emerging Adulthood

University years represent a period of identity exploration, independence, and development of intimate relationships.

Arnett (2000); Collins (2003) — see References.

2

Relationship Status and Self-Worth

Romantic relationships may become connected with feelings of acceptance, belonging, and personal value. However, relationship status itself does not automatically determine self-worth — the meaning individuals attach to relationships is what matters.

3

Social Expectations and Relationship Norms

Young adults' ideas about relationships are shaped by family, peers, culture, and social expectations. Being single or partnered may carry different meanings depending on the social environment.

4

Identity Construction Through Social Interaction

People develop meanings about themselves and the world through social interactions.

Mead (1934); Blumer (1969) — see References.

Although previous research examines romantic relationships and psychological outcomes, less attention has been given to how university students themselves construct meanings around relationships and how these meanings influence identity and self-worth.
